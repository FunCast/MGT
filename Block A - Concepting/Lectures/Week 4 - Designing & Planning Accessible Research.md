# Accessibility
## What do we mean with accessibility?
The practice of making information, activities, and environments sensible, meaningful, and usable for as many people as possible

Disability could be:
- Vision
- Movement
- Thinking
- Learning
- Communicating
- Hearing
- Remembering
- Mental Health
- Social relationships

Access needs that aren't a diagnosis:
- Age
- Language
- Education
- Economic position
- Technological aptitude

Disabilities don't have to be permanent

|       | Permanent                  | Temp                       | Situational                                  |
| ----- | -------------------------- | -------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| See   | Blind                      | Concussion or migraine<br> | Glare on a screen or a tiny handheld display |
| Move  | Limb difference            | A broken arm               | One-handed because you're holding something  |
| Hear  | Deaf or hard of hearing    | An ear infection           | Loud surroundings                            |
| Speak | Non-verbal or non-speaking | Inflamed vocal cords       | Loud surroundings or a heavy accent          |
Some accessible tools are made for some, and used by all. Examples are; subtitles, remappable controls, colorblind modes

## Two biases:
#### False consensus effect
Cognitive bias that makes you think everyone else is the same as you.
Assume play testers see, hear, and move through the game the way you do.
Fix: Recruit across access needs, background and abilities, not just the easiest sample to reach.
#### Principle of least effort
Behavioural hupothesis where peaople gravitate to whateve takes least energy.
Assuming players will push solve a problem the same way you did while building the game.
Fix: Ask what workarounds they already use elsewhere, and plan for real breaks into your sessions.

## Measuring accesibility
There is no single "correct" way to implement or measure

#### Principles, laws & guidelines
- Natural accessibility laws (often on government sites)
- General guidelines, (ISO, W3C/WCAG)
- Game specific: Game Accessibility Guidelines
- Media specific: BBC Editorial Guidelines

#### Real world usability
- Direct feedback from players with the access need
- Observed friction points during testing
- Usability finding often feed back into future guidelines
- [[POUR principle]]

[[POUR principle]]
A framework that tells your something has to e Perceivable, Operable, Understandable, Robust

Perceivable - Subtitles and audio cues for the same in game event
Operable - Remappable controls, aim assist, adjustable difficulty
Understandable - Clear UI language, consistent icons and prompts
Robust - Compatibility with screen readers and assistive controllers

## 4 steps to planning accessible research
- Goal - What is the access need and who has it?
- Participants - Recruitments, ethics, how you'll reach them
- Timeline - Build in more time than you think
- Resources - Hardware, assistive tech, travel, budget

Consider the goal
- What is the access need and who has it?
- What guidelines or research already exist?
- How does your research actually benefit them?

Consider the participants
- Recruitment is often harder, Plan more time!
- Online vs. in person, and can they reach you?
- You'll almost always need ethics board approval

Consider the timeline
- Budget 30-50% more time than typical research
- Recruitment, interviews, and analysis may take longer
- Run a pilot test before your real data collection

Consider the resources
- Assistive tech: screen readers, braille input, switches
- Reference existing solutions such as AbleToPlay
- Travel and budget for participants who need support

# Research problems
## Exploratory
What is X?
How do people talk about X?
## Descriptive
1 variable at a time
How much X is there?
What are the components/dimentions of X?
## Corrolational / assosiative
What is the relationship between X and Y?
Do units with more X also have more Y?
## Causel / explanitory
Does X cause/influence Y?
Does (moderator) moderate/change the relationship between X and Y?
Does (mediater) mediate/explaain the relationship between X and Y?

## Testing
#### Crossectional
Many cases, many variables, one point in time.
Can not answer causel questions.

#### Longtitudinal
Multiple occasions with fewer people.
Still includes multiple cases and variables.

#### Experimental
Manipulated variables by assigning different version to test cause and effect.
Minimum of 30 cases.
