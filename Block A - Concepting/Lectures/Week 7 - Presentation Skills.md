## Our [Block A presentation]
5 min setup -> 15 min presentation -> 5 min discussion
Talking fast is not a solution to lack of time for everything you want to talk about, condense the information

Keep your slides simple, don't give an information overload
Show what your topic is right at the start (splash screen/first slide), informing whether people they are at the right presentation.
Use appealing visuals. Let information enter one by one using animations

Introduce your research, why are you doing this and why should people care?

Never assume people know things (example MDA model) explain it using graphics
Use big important people from the industry to prove a point

> Being able to explain a complex topic with simple words is masterful.

Simplify and do the grandma test. Use LLM's to say "explain it to me like I'm a 6 year old"
In [Block A] you can already talk about your methodology, but keep it short to keep focus on your research question and Literature review. It is not a process report, focus on the research, what you find and how you plan to use it. If you talk about process it has to be relevant on why choices are made and what fascinating information this tells.
## Presenting at conferences
Talk to the conference organizer first and see what the level of professionalism is at this conference before preparing and holding your presentation. Also inform here how much prior knowledge their general audience has, allowing you to skip information that the audience will already know, and still keep all base information needed to explain your topic to the audience.

The questions slide doesn't have to be the final slide, sometimes preparing additional slides for expected questions can show professionalism in the fact you pre-prepared for the questions, and including visuals to questions you're answering.
## Dealing with the Q&A. 
Not every conference has a Q&A. When they are includede prepare for them. Think ahead on what they may ask.
> "That is a super complicated question that we don't have time for in this time slot. Maybe we can talk about this after the presentation." (run before they actually get to ask to dodge the question)

If you can't avoid the question, it's better not to bullshit, but instead take the hit and admit you haven't thought of it and that you'll have to think about it.
"I think/guess/speculate" will allow you to show that it is not a concrete answer, ensuring the audience knows not to take the information at face value.

Separate the "thank yo for your time" and "are there any questions". This avoids people applauding while others are trying to answer questions. Do questions after the "end of talk" in case of conferences where you would otherwise go over time. Allowing to skip the questions ensures you stay within the time slot.

Include references in your slides. Give credits where credits are due. If people ask you need to be ready to answer. Describe it in APA style.
Argue why you chose certain references, why is this one relevant in comparison to certain other writers.
